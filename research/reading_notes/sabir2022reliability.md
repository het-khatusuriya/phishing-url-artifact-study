## Problem
The robustness and reliability of ML phishing-URL detectors against cheap adversarial URLs is largely unknown.

## Method
Methodology to test reliability and robustness: adversarial URL generator (URLBUG), reproduction of 50 traditional-ML and DL detectors, evaluation on Advdata

## Dataset(s)
Reproduces 50 ML phishing-URL detectors; phishing URLs from PhishTank/OpenPhish/ISCXURL2016/PhishStorm (96,693); legitimate URLs crawled from 1,500 Alexa domains to obtain full paths; adversarial set 'Advdata' generated by URLBUG

## Features
Lexical, host-based and DL (char/word) representations as in the reproduced papers

## Evaluation protocol
Baseline (balanced, in-distribution) vs adversarial URLs; MCC as main metric; registration-cost and real-world-use analysis of adversarial domains

## Metrics reported
Median MCC falls from 0.92 (baseline) to 0.02 on Advdata; adversarial domains registrable at a median $11.99/yr; 63.94% of already-registered ones were used maliciously (abstract).

## Main findings
State-of-the-art URL detectors are brittle; the training vocabulary is biased toward phishing target brand tokens. Explicitly notes that using Alexa index pages as the legitimate class 'creates a bias in the data' because phishing feeds provide full URLs while Alexa provides only index pages.

## Limitations
**Authors' stated:** Adversarial generation is heuristic; reproduction may not match original hyper-parameters.

**Our caveats:** Baseline evaluation remains balanced and random-split; no domain grouping.

## Research gap
Combining robustness testing with artefact ablation and domain-grouped splits.

## Relevance to our project
Peer-reviewed statement of the index-page/full-URL sampling artefact plus a reproducible robustness protocol; strongly supports our artifact-removal and robustness sections.
